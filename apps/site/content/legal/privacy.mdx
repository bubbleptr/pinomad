This policy covers the official PiNomad server at `app.pinomad.si`, run by PiNomad's author, and this website, `pinomad.si`. PiNomad is open source and anyone can run their own server. If you use a server someone else runs, including one you run yourself, its operator is responsible for it and this policy doesn't apply.

## In short

- Your agent runs on your own machine (the host). Conversations, files and commands travel between your devices and your host end-to-end encrypted. The server forwards them as ciphertext and can't read them.
- The server stores your account (your email address, a password hash if you set a password, and the Google, GitHub or Apple sign-ins you linked), the hosts on your account, your sign-in sessions, the devices you sign in on and which hosts they joined, and push registrations from the iPhone app.
- Your email address is used to sign you in, to send you codes and to tell you about changes to your account's security. No newsletters, no analytics, no ads. Nothing is sold, and nothing is shared beyond the services named under "Services that handle some of your data".
- When you delete your account, the server's records of it go with it, and the server asks Apple to revoke PiNomad's access to your Apple ID.

## What the server stores

All of this lives in one database on the server.

- **Account:** your email address and when it was verified, your password as a scrypt hash (never the password itself; an account that only signs in with Google, GitHub or Apple has none), a random account ID that your apps use to tell your pairings from another account's on the same device, and when the account was created. An account created before email sign-up existed keeps its username until an email address is added to it.
- **Sign-in methods:** for each Google, GitHub or Apple sign-in you linked: which provider, the ID that provider gives you (a number or opaque string, not your email or username there), the email address it reported, and when you linked it. For Apple, also the name you chose to share the first time you signed in, if any, and a token Apple issued that the server keeps only so it can revoke PiNomad's access when you unlink Apple or delete your account.
- **Emailed codes:** for each code the server mails you (to sign up, reset your password, add an email address or confirm an action): a hash of the code, what it's for, the email address, the account it belongs to if there is one yet, how many wrong guesses it has had, and when it expires.
- **Sign-ins through Google, GitHub or Apple in progress:** while you're on the provider's pages, the state that ties the provider's answer to your browser or app. If no account matches when you come back, the server also holds what the provider reported (the ID, the email address and, for Apple, the name and token) while you choose to create an account or link it to one you have. The secrets your browser or app holds for these steps are stored only as SHA-256 hashes.
- **Confirmations:** when you confirm an action with your password or an emailed code, a hash of the short-lived confirmation the server gives your session.
- **Sign-in sessions:** one for each browser or app you sign in on: a SHA-256 hash of the session token, when it was issued and when it expires.
- **Hosts on your account:** for each host you join with `pinomad join`: its ID, its public key (used to set up the encrypted channel), its display name (by default the machine's hostname), when it joined and when it last connected.
- **Devices on your account:** for each browser or app that signs in: an ID the app generated at random on that device (it isn't derived from the hardware), the name the app gives itself (such as "Mac Chrome", "Mac Desktop" or "iPhone"), when it first and last signed in, and, if you removed it, when. The server uses this to list your devices and to remove one from your hosts.
- **Devices on your hosts:** when a device pairs with a host through your account, which device asked, which host, and when the host's invite expires. Once the host admits the device it reports the public key it admitted it under, and the server keeps that key so it can later ask the host to revoke it. After you remove a device, the server also keeps each revocation a host hasn't confirmed yet.
- **Host join commands:** the one-time command from **Add a host**, stored as a SHA-256 hash with the account that made it, when it was made, when it expires and when it was used.
- **Hosts waiting to join:** when you run `pinomad join` without a token, the host's ID, its name, its system (such as "macOS (arm64)") and the IP address it asked from, so the page where you enter the code can show you which machine you are adding. Both codes are stored as SHA-256 hashes. The name, system and IP address are deleted as soon as you confirm or deny the request, and at the latest when it expires after 10 minutes.
- **Push registrations:** for an iPhone with push turned on: the device token Apple gave the app, whether it's a production or development build, the account it belongs to, which device on your account registered it, and when the app last refreshed it. The server uses this to check that a host only sends notifications to devices signed in to the same account.

## What the server can't see

- **Your work.** Prompts, model replies, files, tool calls, terminal output and your model provider sign-ins stay on your host and your devices. Between them, everything goes over an end-to-end encrypted channel (Noise). When a device can't reach your host directly, the server forwards that channel's ciphertext and keeps none of it.
- **Notification content.** A push notification carries only the conversation title and the event (waiting for your answer, finished, failed), never the question or the reply. The title passes through the server and Apple's push service on its way to your phone. The server doesn't store it.
- **Pairing details.** When you pair a device through your account, the server passes the host's one-time invite and its network addresses to your device and keeps neither the invite nor the addresses (only the record described under "Devices on your hosts").

## What passes through without being kept

- **IP and email addresses.** Like any server, it sees the address of each connection. It keeps IP addresses in memory to limit sign-in, sign-up, code and host-join attempts, and email addresses to limit how many codes go to one address; those counters last up to an hour and are gone when the server restarts. Addresses aren't written to the server's own log, and the database only holds the IP address of a host waiting to join (see above).
- **Logs.** The server's log records when it starts; for each push notification, the host ID, the build type, the result and how long it took; and failures: an email that couldn't be sent (only what it was for, not the address or the code), a sign-in through Google, GitHub or Apple that failed (the provider and the error), and an Apple revocation that failed. It doesn't log notification content, push tokens, passwords, codes or connections. Logs stay on the server and are rotated by the system.

## Email the server sends

Only these, each to your account's address (or, for a sign-up, the address you entered):

- A code to sign up, to reset your password, to add an email address, or to confirm an action such as deleting your account.
- When someone tries to sign up with an address that already has an account: a note saying so, instead of a code.
- A notice when a Google, GitHub or Apple sign-in was linked to your account automatically because it reported the same verified email address, and, sent to the old address, when your account's email address changes.

## Services that handle some of your data

- **Email delivery:** the official server sends mail through Resend, which sees the recipient address and the message (including a code) on its way to you.
- **Google, GitHub and Apple:** only if you sign in with them. The provider knows you signed in to PiNomad. The server asks for your email address and your ID there (Google and GitHub also offer your basic profile, Apple the name you choose to share) and keeps only the ID, the email address and, for Apple, that name.
- **Apple Push Notification service:** see "What the server can't see" and "Where data is stored".

## The server can vouch for new devices

When you sign in on a new device and pair it with a host through your account, the host accepts the device because the server vouches for it. That makes the server operator a trusted party:

- Whoever runs the server, or anyone who breaks into it, could add a device to a host on your account. That device would have the same access as yours, which includes a shell on the host.
- They could also give a device that is pairing for the first time a fake host key and read that device's traffic.
- Devices that are already paired aren't affected, and a device you revoke on a host stays revoked.

If you don't want to extend that trust, turn off **Accept devices from your account** in the host's Devices settings, so that only QR-code pairing works, or run your own server. The reasoning behind this design is in [ADR-0022](https://github.com/bubbleptr/pinomad/blob/main/docs/adr/0022-server-accounts-and-vouching.md) (in Chinese).

## How long things are kept

- **Account:** until you delete it.
- **Sign-in methods:** until you unlink them or delete your account. The last way to sign in can't be unlinked.
- **Emailed codes:** 10 minutes, or until used; a code dies after five wrong guesses. Expired ones are cleared within a minute.
- **Sign-ins through a provider in progress:** 10 minutes at most, including what a provider reported while you choose what to do with it; the one-time code an app trades for a session lasts 2 minutes. Cleared within a minute after they expire.
- **Confirmations:** 5 minutes, or until the session that got them ends.
- **Hosts:** until you run `pinomad leave` on the host, join it to another account, or delete your account.
- **Sessions:** 30 days from sign-in, never extended. Signing out removes that session; **Sign out everywhere** removes all of them; changing your password removes all but the one you're using.
- **Devices on your account:** until you delete your account. A removed device stays listed as removed. An unnamed entry from an older app is cleared at a later sign-in once it has no session and no host record.
- **Devices on your hosts:** a pairing the host never reports is cleared a day after its invite expires. The rest stay until the host leaves your account (`pinomad leave`), joins another account, or you delete your account. A pending revocation is removed when the host confirms it.
- **Host join commands:** they expire after 10 minutes, and expired ones are cleared the next time anyone makes one.
- **Hosts waiting to join:** the host's name, system and IP address go when you confirm or deny the request; the rest goes once the host has its answer. A request nobody answers expires after 10 minutes and is cleared within a minute after that.
- **Push registrations:** removed when you turn push off or sign out on the phone, remove the phone under **Devices**, use **Sign out everywhere**, change your password, delete your account, or when Apple reports the token is no longer valid. A registration the app hasn't refreshed for 90 days is ignored and then cleared. An account keeps at most 32.
- **Rate limits** (sign-in, sign-up, codes, joining hosts): up to an hour, in memory only.

## Deleting your account

You can delete your account at any time; the server asks for your password first, or, for an account without one, a code it emails you. If your app doesn't offer the option yet, contact us (see Contact).

Deleting the account removes, in one step, your account and email address, its sign-in methods, its sessions, its devices and their records on your hosts, its host join commands, the bindings of your hosts, its emailed codes and its push registrations. If you linked Apple, the server asks Apple to revoke PiNomad's access; if that fails, the deletion still goes ahead and the failure is logged. Your hosts are disconnected from the server right away and can't connect through it again until you join them to another account.

What it doesn't touch: the list of paired devices on each host. Each host keeps its own list and is the one that decides who gets in, so devices stay paired there until you revoke them in the host's Devices page. To have your hosts revoke a device first, remove it under **Devices** in the account panel before deleting the account. Everything else on your hosts is yours and was never on the server.

## This website

`pinomad.si` is a set of static pages served by Cloudflare Pages, which handles each request and sees the usual request details, such as your IP address and browser. The site has no analytics and loads nothing from other sites; fonts are served from the site itself.

- The web app at `app.pinomad.si` sets one cookie on `pinomad.si`, `pinomad_app=1`, in browsers that have used it. It holds no account information; it only makes the site's **Sign in** link read **Open web app** and send the home page straight to the app.
- The docs remember your light or dark theme in your browser's local storage.
- The web app itself keeps you signed in with a session cookie that scripts can't read, and stores your paired hosts in the browser's local storage, along with the random device ID it sends when you sign in. During a sign-in through Google, GitHub or Apple it also sets a cookie scripts can't read that ties the provider's answer to your browser; it lasts 10 minutes.

## Where data is stored

The server runs on a virtual server in Los Angeles, in the United States. Push notifications go through Apple's push service, which Apple runs under its own privacy policy.
